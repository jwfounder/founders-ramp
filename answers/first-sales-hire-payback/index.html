How long does a first sales hire take to pay back?

By Justin Weller · Published October 9, 2026 · Updated October 18, 2026

Short answer

Usually longer than founders expect. In three worked examples on our calculator, closed revenue (gross profit) passes the hire's cost in month 15 to 26. Cash collected passes it in month 25 to 34. Before that, the hire digs a cash hole of $200K or more. Plan your runway for the cash line.

What about "6 to 12 months"?

Ask Google how long a first sales hire takes to pay back, and its AI Overview will usually say 6 to 12 months. In the calculator's starting example (example 1 below), closed revenue (gross profit) passes the hire's cost in month 22, and cash collected passes it in month 34.

Two things set those dates:

  • Ramp and sales cycle. Month one is onboarding. The hire then speeds up each month and reaches full speed in month 6. Each month's work signs three months later, so the first signatures land in month 5, and the signed total reaches one full $30K deal in month 7.
  • When customers pay. On monthly billing, each deal's cash comes in over the year after it signs.

Of the one-step changes the calculator tests, billing a year up front does the most. In the starting example, it alone moves cash collected from month 34 to month 22, the same month as closed revenue (gross profit), and shrinks the valley, the biggest gap between cost and cash collected, from about $282K to about $151K.

Does a first sales hire pay back in 6 to 12 months?

Not in our three worked examples. The fastest is month 15 on closed revenue (gross profit) and month 25 on cash collected. Cash collected gets to month 12 when the hire signs about 3.5 times their pay and customers pay a year up front.

What would it take to pay back in 12 months?

The hire in the starting example signs about $403K a year, about 1.8 times their $220K pay. At about $770K a year, 3.5 times their pay, closed revenue (gross profit) passes cost in month 12, but on monthly billing cash collected waits until month 23.

Two lines, and the valley between them

The payback calculator draws what the hire costs you each month, added up. Then it draws two lines for what they bring back.

Closed revenue (gross profit) counts each new contract, after gross margin, the month it is signed.

Cash collected counts the same gross profit when the customer actually pays. If customers pay monthly, a signed $30K deal comes in over 12 months.

The valley is the biggest gap between cost and cash collected. It is the money you need in the bank to carry the hire.

1. The calculator's starting numbers

$220K pay at target, plus 20% for benefits and taxes, a $25K hiring fee and $500 a month in tools. That is $22,500 a month, or $270K a year. Five months to get up to speed, a three-month sales cycle, $30K deals paid monthly, 8 live deals a month, a 20% win rate, and the hire hits 70% of that. 80% gross margin, 12% of customers lost each year.

  • First full deal lands in month 7.
  • Closed revenue (gross profit) passes cost in month 22.
  • Cash collected passes cost in month 34.
  • The valley is about $282K, at month 17.

2. Bigger deals, paid a year up front

Same seller and costs. $50K deals billed a year up front, a five-month sales cycle, 5 live deals a month, and six months to get up to speed.

  • First full deal lands in month 10.
  • Closed revenue (gross profit) and cash collected both pass cost in month 26, because the customer pays the year when they sign.
  • The valley is about $203K, at month 10.

3. Industry averages from The Bridge Group

$200K pay at target, a 6.2-month ramp and an $875K annual quota, the figures The Bridge Group reports in its 2026 AE report. Everything else stays at the starting numbers, including hitting 70% of quota. The Bridge Group also reports that only 48% of AEs hit quota in 2026.

  • Closed revenue (gross profit) passes cost in month 15.
  • Cash collected passes cost in month 25.
  • The valley is about $228K, at month 13.

What this means before you hire

In example 1, the hire looks paid for in month 22 if you only count signed deals, and you are still a year away on cash. That year is what runs founders out of money.

Count the valley against your runway, not your plan. Then look at what pulls payback in: billing up front, a shorter cycle, a faster ramp, or handing the hire live deals on day one. Common follow-ups

How long does a first sales hire take to pay back?

In three worked examples on the calculator, closed revenue (gross profit) passes the hire's cost in month 15 to 26, and cash collected passes it in month 25 to 34. Ramp time, sales cycle, deal size and how customers pay move it most.

When does a new sales hire break even?

On closed revenue (gross profit), month 15 to 26 in our three worked examples. On cash collected, month 25 to 34. Ramp time, sales cycle, deal size and how customers pay move it most.

What is the valley?

The biggest cash hole the hire digs before cash collected catches up with what they cost. In the calculator's starting example it is about $282K, at month 17.

Why measure cash collected and not just closed revenue?

Closed revenue counts a deal the month it is signed. If customers pay monthly, the cash arrives over the next year. You pay the seller in cash, so cash collected tells you whether you can afford the hire.

What shortens payback the most?

In these examples, billing a year up front closes the gap between closed revenue and cash. A faster ramp, a shorter sales cycle, and handing the hire live deals on day one also pull payback in.
