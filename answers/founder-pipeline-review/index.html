How should a founder run a weekly pipeline review?

By Justin Weller · Published October 30, 2026

Short answer

Once a week, look at every open deal: close date, stage, amount, next step, forecast category. Inspect the last call, not the activity count. Ask what the deal is, who cares, how they decide, what happens next, and whether the buyer said it. Then coach the behavior, soon and specifically.

When you have sellers, you inspect deals. You coach behavior. You do not inspect hope. A pipeline review is where that happens every week.

Look at every open deal

Close date, stage, amount, next step, forecast category. Inspect the last call, not the activity count. Rigor isn't more activity.

Ask five questions of each deal

What is this? Purpose, stage, next step. If they can't say it in a minute, it's not live.

Who cares? Economic buyer. Champion. Who is hurt if this slips. Multi-thread or it's a story.

How they decide. Success criteria they will actually accept. Metrics. Proof. Not the slide you wish they used.

What happens next. Owner, date, what good looks like on both sides. Equal commitment, or call it early.

Did they say it? If the buyer didn't say it, it's not real. A stage click is not confirmation.

Coach the behavior, then back to the goals

Goals on one page, agreed before the week starts. If they hit it, praise the behavior, soon, specifically. If they miss, correct the behavior, not the person. Then return to the goals. The cadence is from Ken Blanchard and Spencer Johnson, The One Minute Manager. See inspect a live deal.

Common follow-ups

When is a deal not real?

When the seller can't say what it is in a minute, or the buyer never said it. A stage click is not confirmation.

Do I need a pipeline review before my first hire?

Yes. If you can't explain how you qualify, inspect, and advance a deal in plain language, you're not ready to hire. Read The first hire freezes the process.
