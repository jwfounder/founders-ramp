Fractional sales leader vs VP of Sales

Short answer

If you don't have a written, repeatable sales motion yet, a full-time VP of Sales usually walks into a blank page, and that seat is expensive to get wrong. Build the motion first, by you with outside help. Make your first sales hire a frontline manager with launch experience who can grow into the VP seat.

The founder's hired a VP of Sales from a big-name company. Day one, the VP asks for the playbook, the comp plan, the ideal customer profile, and a team to manage. There's none of that. There's the founder's memory and a CRM nobody updates.

Six months later the VP's built enterprise process for a company that hasn't found its repeatable sale. It looks like growth. It's often a bigger version of the same empty deal.

What a VP of Sales is for

A good VP scales a motion. They hire managers, set territories and quotas, build the forecast the board trusts, and grow a team that already knows how to win. They're at their best when there's something to scale.

What you need before that

Someone has to answer the earlier questions. Who buys, and why now. What the first call does. What has to be true before a deal is real. How the first seller gets hired, paid, trained, and inspected. That's building work, and it's usually done once, well, by someone who's done it before.

Make the first hire a frontline manager

Most VPs and CROs don't have the zero to one experience an early company needs. If they do, it's at least five years old, often ten. You need that muscle now. Hire a talented frontline sales manager who has the potential to become your VP or CRO. It's even better if they've launched a new product before, at another startup, as an individual contributor who got promoted, or inside a larger company.

Either they grow into the VP or CRO seat, or at some point they step back. That's how it worked at Workiva, where Justin was a founding salesperson. The first sales hire was an excellent frontline manager.

Where Founders Ramp fits

We build the motion with you, find and interview your first sales leader, install commission, training, and structure, and teach you how to inspect the work. That's Installing the team. How to decide

Can you write down how you qualify, inspect, and advance a deal? If not, you need the motion before the VP.

Are deals closing without you on the call? If not, a VP will inherit your bottleneck.

Do you have sellers for a VP to lead? If the answer is zero or one, hire a frontline manager who can grow into the VP seat, not a VP.

Still not sure? When to hire your first AE covers the seat before this one.

Common follow-ups

Should an early-stage startup hire a VP of Sales?

No, not as your first sales hire. Hire a frontline manager with launch experience who can grow into the VP or CRO seat, and build the motion with them first.

When does a VP of Sales make sense?

When there's a motion that works, deals close without the founder on every call, and there are sellers for the VP to lead. Ideally that's the manager you hired first.
