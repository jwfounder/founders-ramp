How much equity should a founding AE get?

By Justin Weller · Published November 8, 2026

Short answer

In our October 30, 2026 snapshot of 16 US startup first-sales-hire postings, 15 mention equity. The 8 that state a percentage range span 0.10% to 1.00%. This is a small, mixed-role sample of advertised ranges. It establishes neither a market median nor a recommended grant. Set the offer against the role, cash compensation and grant terms. A title alone is insufficient.

A founding title does not set the equity grant. Start with what the seller will own, what cash you can pay and what the proposed grant actually means.

What the advertised equity ranges show

Our founding AE salary snapshot records 16 US startup first-sales-hire postings as of October 30, 2026. Of those, 14 use a founding AE title; the other two are a founding BDR and a founding field sales rep. Across the full sample, 15 mention equity and 8 state a percentage range. Those ranges span 0.10% to 1.00%. Do not present that mixed-role range as a founding-AE-only benchmark.

Here are four founding AE examples from that dated snapshot. These ranges describe advertisements. Accepted grants were not recorded. Company links point to the underlying postings; the snapshot preserves what we recorded at the time.

What the current tracker can and cannot tell you

The live First Sales Hire Tracker, checked November 8, 2026, contains 6 verified founding-sales openings. All 6 mention equity. It is a narrower shortlist of first-seller or founder-led-sales-transition roles, excluding existing sales teams and ambiguous first-hire claims. It is not directly comparable with the older 16-posting snapshot. A smaller list does not mean ten jobs closed, and it does not establish a change in equity offers.

Neither sample tells us what a candidate negotiated, the eventual grant size, the vesting schedule or the value at exit. We have not calculated a median grant from range endpoints.

Compare the work before comparing the percentage

Write down whether this person inherits qualified demand and a working sales process, or must also find buyers and build the playbook. A first closer and a first sales leader are different jobs. Use the founding AE job description to define the seat before pricing it.

Then compare cash and equity separately. An unlabeled salary range is not necessarily base pay or on-target earnings. A dollar equity figure is not interchangeable with an ownership percentage: Clark's $60K to $200K equity figure in the October 30 snapshot is reported in dollars. We have made no percentage conversion.

Ask what the grant actually buys

Before presenting an offer, get the grant type, share or option count, ownership denominator, vesting conditions and required approvals in writing. For options, clarify exercise terms and costs. Ask how future financing could dilute ownership. Have counsel explain the grant documents; this page is not legal or tax advice.

Do not count an uncertain equity outcome as cash available to cover a seller's living costs or your startup's payroll. Check the full compensation cost in the first hire payback calculator, and read when to hire your first AE before posting the role.

Common follow-ups

Do founding AEs usually get equity?

In the October 30, 2026 snapshot, 15 of 16 first-sales-hire postings mention equity. The narrower live tracker checked November 8, 2026 has 6 openings, and all 6 mention equity. The samples differ in scope. They cannot establish a market trend.

Is 1% the standard founding AE grant?

No. 1.00% is the upper endpoint of the percentage ranges advertised in our October 30 snapshot, and does not establish the typical award. The snapshot does not establish a market median or what candidates actually received.

Can I compare a dollar equity range with a percentage?

Not directly. Clark advertised equity as $60K to $200K in the October 30 snapshot. Ask how the dollar figure was calculated and what ownership percentage the proposed grant represents before comparing it with a percentage offer.

What should a founder clarify before making an equity offer?

Clarify the grant type, number of shares or options, ownership denominator, vesting conditions, exercise terms where relevant and approvals still needed. Review the full cash package alongside the equity, and have counsel explain the actual grant documents.
