What should a founder do on the first sales call?

By Justin Weller · Published October 18, 2026

Short answer

Find out why they'd buy. After a short intro, name the usual reasons companies buy something like yours and ask which one is top of mind. The answer tells you which problem to work on, and how well they answer tells you whether this person can make things happen.

A lot of first calls turn into a tour of the product. The buyer listens to your spiel, watches the demo, and wishes you luck. They were curious about what's out there, and nothing in the call gave them a reason to change.

The job of the first call is to get to a real business reason, not "check out my stuff."

Open with why companies buy

After a short intro, I tell the buyer the usual reasons companies buy something like this: to move faster, to lower a risk, to deliver on an AI strategy mandate, to do more with the same people, or to get a cost under control. Then I ask, "Which of these is top of mind for you?"

The answer tells you which problem to work on. How well they answer tells you whether this person knows what's going on in the business and can make things happen.

Attach to the problem, by person

Same deal, different pain. The person doing the work wants less grind. Their manager wants the team to hit the number. The executive wants proof a new way of working will stick. Ask about what it costs them now, what good looks like, and how they'll know. See problem-first discovery.

Ask the hard questions now

On the first call there's no risk for the buyer. Later, they're negotiating, and the same question gets a different answer. Get the really hard questions out at the beginning.

Handle what you hear

"We already use something for that." Good. What happens after the data leaves that system? Who picks it up, how long does it take, and what happened the last time it went wrong? See We already use something for that.

"Call me in six months." Why six? What would have to be true for it to be now? Never trash the other option. Sell what's actually different. More lines and answers are on what you hear on the first call.

End with a next step they own

A calendar hold is not a next step. It's a way to end the call without saying no. Ask for the same commitment you're giving: who owns the decision, what good looks like, what happens if the next meeting goes well. And ask who else has to be comfortable with this before it moves. That's equal commitment.

Common follow-ups

What should a founder say first on a sales call?

After a short intro, name the usual reasons companies buy something like yours and ask which one is top of mind. Their answer tells you which problem to work on.

How do I answer "we already use something for that"?

Don't compare features. Ask what happens after the data leaves that system, who picks it up, how long it takes, and what happened the last time it went wrong.

How should a first sales call end?

With a next step the buyer owns: a date, who owns the decision, and what good looks like. A calendar hold booked to end the call is not a next step.
