What are you measuring?

The dashboard is what already happened. The forecast is the work.

In short

The dashboard is what already happened. The forecast is the work.

Let's be clear: most sales dashboards measure what already happened. They do not tell you what is going to happen, nor afford you an opportunity to impact that future.

Walk into a war room. New opportunities. Closed revenue. Meetings booked. Win rate. The wall moves, so it feels like management. It isn't. Those numbers are the past tense. Somebody already said yes, or they didn't. You cannot manage a thing that is already over.

Forecasts are not a prettier scoreboard. Forecasts are about actions and activity. What a person will actually do. If your forecast is only a color, you do not have a forecast. You have a memory with a date on it.

You cannot control whether someone agrees to meet you today. You can want it. You can put it on a slide. Staring at it will not produce it.

What you can control is the work. Activities. Actions. Motion. Thoughtfulness. How many times you ask is one example, not the whole job. Who you ask and how you ask are examples too. Under the waterline it is a lot more than that.

Prospecting time that actually makes the calendar. A list of people who can buy, not a sequence that fired itself. Call prep. Research. Writing the problem in their words. Asking for a time. Asking for a name. Following up the same day. Listening to the call. Protecting the block. Skipping the fake meeting. Killing the dead deal. A next step they own. Being honest about risk. Practicing the open. Showing up on time. Going two-up in the account. Starving busywork. Putting your neck out.

I asked one of my top people if she had enough pipeline to hit her number. Goal was $400,000. She had about $350,000 marked qualified. She didn't have time to prospect. Training, Salesforce, forecast tools, incoming everything. Her customer calls were in the calendar. Her kids' games were in the calendar. Prospecting was not. The gap was not a motivation problem. It was an activity that never got a square on Tuesday.

We have all done the other version. Fill the room. Count the tours. Two hundred demos. Zero customers. Motion that is not correlated still feels like work. The dashboard will thank you. The quarter will not.

If you lead people, stop managing the past. Name the activities a rep can actually control this week. Get discrete. Put them on the calendar. Inspect whether they happened. Starve the rest, even when the rest has a chart.

The dashboard is what already happened. The forecast is the work.
