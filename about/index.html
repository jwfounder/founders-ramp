Why Founders Ramp exists

Many founders have a real product, a real buyer, and a real reason someone should talk to them. The market still does not always know what they do, when to call, or why a conversation should happen now.

Referrals dry up. Founder-led selling becomes a bottleneck. Outbound gets ignored. Content creates visibility but not a next step. Founders Ramp exists to put a sales motion under that, so the company can sell before it hires sellers.

Justin Weller

I am Justin Weller. Founders Ramp is me, in the room, with a founder who still has to close.

At Workiva I was on the original sales team. Zero customers. The job was to find who would buy, why they would buy now, and a motion other people could run. That included companies getting ready to go public, and later mid-market financial services.

I learned that first in wireless. New markets. New teams. Then the same work in software. A market that is not selling needs a process, managers who inspect work, and a number that is not a wish.

At Smartsheet I stood up a Global 2000 key accounts motion. It failed. I keep that in the room. Founders copy enterprise theater because it looks like growth. It is often just a bigger version of the same empty deal.

I have also taken over consulting practices that were not selling. Same holes: no discovery, no qualification, no equal commitment, hope instead of a next step with a date.

How the work is different

We start with the core sale: what you sell, who it is for, why they care, and what trigger creates demand. Then a repeatable motion that fits the people who will actually run it.

The goal is not activity. The goal is qualified conversations, a next step someone owns, and a way to inspect the work once you have a first hire.

We do not become your VP. You still have to own the number.
