Buyers and markets 2026-11-10

71% of B2B buyers have chosen a safer supplier over one they believed offered better value

VML released The Future Buyer Report 2026 on November 10, 2026. It surveyed 750 enterprise B2B buyers across seven industries in eight markets, including the United States, between late July and early August 2026. 55% of buyers begin supplier research with generative AI tools, compared with 45% who start with traditional search engines. 73% believe an underperforming purchase could affect their career, and 71% have chosen a safer supplier over one they believed offered better value. 38% of significant buying initiatives end without a decision. VML calls this defensibility bias. A founder selling against a known incumbent can make the choice easier to defend: proof from similar customers, a small first commitment and a clear plan for what happens if it misses.

Founder-led sales 2026-11-10

Sales is 18.4% of headcount at AI hypergrowth companies, close to 20.7% in the rest of tech

Jason Lemkin wrote on SaaStr on November 10, 2026 about new data from Pave's compensation database of more than 10,000 companies. The median share of the workforce in sales is 18.4% at AI hypergrowth companies, 20.7% in the rest of tech and 10.4% at non-technology companies. Customer support is 1.8% of headcount at AI hypergrowth companies against 4.4% in the rest of tech, and engineering is 47% against 33.9%. Lemkin also cites ICONIQ's State of Go-to-Market 2026, where AI-forward teams report 67% quota attainment against 59% for everyone else. His read: these companies have fewer reps because they have fewer employees overall, and sales keeps most of its share. For a founder, AI tools change how a first seller spends the day, and the companies growing fastest still hire sellers.

Buyers and markets 2026-11-10

Only 9% of European industrial companies regularly seek out startups to buy from or partner with

BCG released its study Buy First, Scale Fast on November 10, 2026. Statista surveyed 370 people from business and research for BCG between September 9 and October 16, 2026, including 185 senior industry executives. 38% of executives said their company seeks contact with startups for purchases or partnerships at least occasionally, and 9% said it does so regularly. Three quarters of the companies have tried partnering with young tech firms, and 19% report mostly successful results from repeated work together. Before buying more from tech startups, 51% want to be sure the technology works reliably, 35% want lower financial risk, 31% need compatibility with existing IT systems and 25% want simpler internal procurement and approval. A founder selling to a large company can answer all four up front: a scoped pilot, references, an integration plan and a short path through procurement.

BCG
Founder-led sales 2026-11-09

33% of sales leaders say the best way to coach reps on messaging is to show why it works

The Science of Scaling newsletter by Jay Fuchs, published November 9, 2026, draws on surveys of more than 300 sales leaders, with data sourced through Panoplai. Asked how managers best coach reps to use marketing's messaging, 33% chose showing why an asset works so reps can adapt it themselves, and 32% chose live practice such as role-play and call reviews. Asked for the single biggest barrier to getting more value from AI in prospecting, 32% named reps treating AI as a shortcut. On the habits that separate reps who use marketing well, 28% of first-place votes went to tailoring material to the specific buyer and 23% to knowing what assets exist and where to find them. When a founder hands the pitch to a first seller, walking through why each story wins gives that seller something to adapt on every call.

Science of Scaling
Buyers and markets 2026-11-09

84% of B2B decision-makers used AI to summarize vendors' thought leadership during evaluation

Edelman described its just-released 2026 Edelman-LinkedIn B2B Thought Leadership Impact Report on November 9, 2026. The survey covers more than 1,100 business executives across seven countries. During vendor evaluation, 84% of B2B decision-makers used AI to summarize thought leadership. Of those who shared it, 89% did so through private channels such as email, direct messages, work chats and meetings. 76% say thought leadership discussed in their buying group helped them judge whether a vendor understood their needs, and 59% of final decision-makers say poor or absent thought leadership contributed to a vendor being removed from consideration. Asked what builds the most trust, 56% chose a clear understanding of their challenges and 14% chose the strength of the product. Write about the buyer's problem in enough depth that a summary still carries your point.

AI at work 2026-11-09

42% of CMOs now monitor and correct what AI says about their brand

Gartner released a survey of 408 senior marketing leaders on November 9, 2026, conducted from July through August 2026. 42% of CMOs said they have taken steps to monitor and correct misinformation about their brands in AI-generated output. 61% have adapted content strategies to improve machine readability and visibility in AI-driven experiences, and 94% plan to increase investment in human touchpoints over the next year. Gartner analyst Sharon Cantor Ceurvorst said that as B2B buyers rely on AI tools to discover and evaluate brands, inaccurate information can directly influence perceptions and purchasing decisions. A small team can start simply: ask the main AI assistants what they say about your company, then correct the facts on your own site.

AI answers named brands in 97% of responses, but directly recommended one in only 47.6%

Omniscient Digital published original research on November 6, 2026, analyzing 8,392 AI responses to 300 buying prompts across four AI search platforms. The prompts ran from September 30 to October 6, 2026. At least one brand appeared in 97% of responses, while only 47.6% included a direct recommendation. Before counting an AI mention as a win, check whether the answer actually tells your target buyer to consider you.

42% of enterprise software buyers mainly buy their AI tools, and only 22% of software sellers expect that

West Monroe surveyed 300 U.S. executives in Q3 2026, enterprise software buyers and software sellers, and published the results on November 5, 2026. 42% of buyers say they primarily buy AI solutions, and only 22% of sellers believe their customers primarily buy. Prowi read 2,064 open sales job ads from 776 employers in Europe and North America on November 2, 2026. 23% of the ads mention commission, OTE or variable pay. 40% of employers that advertise commission call it uncapped, only 5 of 198 mention accelerators, and none of the 480 commission ads explain clawbacks.
